1 · The path to churn

Churn is 90 days without an order. We act in the weeks before day 90

An account rarely stops overnight. Its usual order gap stretches, it orders less, and only then does it reach 90 days of silence. Each stage below has its own warning and its own play. Rep time and discounts go before day 90, because winning an account back costs far more than keeping it.

3 · Already churned

After day 90: one automated message, no rep time

Ordered again within the next quarter, without any action

Regular accounts that had already gone 90 days without an order on the backtest date.

Why we don't send reps here

Half of these accounts come back by themselves, so a visit or discount mostly pays for orders that would have come anyway.

The other half needs a real win-back offer, which costs far more than a reorder nudge or a call while the account was still active.

So the play is one automated win-back message with the usual basket. Accounts still silent after 180 days count as lost and move to a reactivation campaign.

Method

How the warnings and the backtest work

  • Account: a zip-code area. Regular account: 3+ orders in the 6-month baseline (days 91–270 before the date). Size groups: 3–5, 6–11 and 12+ baseline orders.
  • Churn: 90 days without an order. An account that has already passed day 90 is churned and leaves the prevention list.
  • Normal rate: baseline orders × market factor (all orders in the last 90 days ÷ all orders in the baseline), which corrects for 2017 growth and season. The account's usual gap is 90 days ÷ that expected number of orders.
  • Overdue: the chance of a gap this long at the account's normal rate is below 10% (Poisson: e−rate × days). Due soon: that chance is 10–30%. Ordering less: last-90-day orders at most half of expected and below a 10% chance level. Dropped line: a category bought in 2+ orders in 2+ baseline months, with no order in the last 90 days.
  • Backtest: warnings set on 31 Dec 2017, 31 Jan, 28 Feb, 31 Mar, 30 Apr and 31 May 2018. Outcome: did the account reach 90 days without an order. Dates overlap, so read pooled numbers as consistent direction, not formal significance; every single date shows the same gap.
  • Ranking: revenue at risk = churn risk for the account's warning and size × quarterly run-rate (baseline revenue ÷ 2). In the 31 May 2018 test the risks came only from Dec 2017 to Feb 2018, whose outcomes were known by then. Today's list uses all six dates.
  • Tested and rejected: a dropped line adds no churn warning once volume is known, even with stricter definitions. Putting warned accounts first regardless of value reached less revenue than risk × run-rate. A regional risk multiplier did not improve the ranking, so geography is used for routes and central fixes.
  • Caveat: here one zip code is many independent buyers. With one outlet owner, a late delivery or a dropped line probably matters more, so test these signals again on HEINEKEN data. Save rates are assumptions until a hold-out group measures them.
